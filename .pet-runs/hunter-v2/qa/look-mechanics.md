# Hunter look mechanics

Hunter is a compact illustrated German Shepherd with a clear muzzle, large expressive eyes, tall ears, a black saddle, tan muzzle/chest/paws, and a grounded four-paw stance.

- Stable anchor: keep the paws, lower torso, scale, and baseline fixed. Do not rotate, skew, or tilt the whole dog.
- Gaze lead: the pupils and muzzle lead each direction; the head follows with a restrained yaw or pitch. The neck and upper chest may follow slightly.
- Follow-through: upright ears remain attached and preserve their shape, with only subtle perspective/occlusion changes. The tail remains stable with minimal natural counterbalance.
- Eyes: preserve the existing eye whites, irises, pupils, eyelids, highlights, and friendly expression as one coherent eye construction. Do not add replacement or googly eyes.
- Motion budget: each 22.5-degree step changes eye position, muzzle direction, and head angle by a small even amount. No scale, baseline, paw, marking, or silhouette jumps.

Cardinal pose families:

- 000 up: pupils and muzzle aim visibly upward; chin lifts slightly; more underside of muzzle is visible; ears remain upright.
- 090 screen-right: pupils and nose tip cross to the screen-right side of the head center; the rightward side of the muzzle leads and the opposite cheek is slightly occluded.
- 180 down: pupils and muzzle aim visibly downward; chin tucks; more brow/top plane is visible while paws remain fixed.
- 270 screen-left: pupils and nose tip cross to the screen-left side of the head center; the leftward side of the muzzle leads and the opposite cheek is slightly occluded.

Diagonals interpolate both required axes evenly between adjacent cardinal families. Keep Hunter friendly and attentive, not startled or aggressive. Preserve the exact black-and-tan markings and clean outlined sprite style. No props, labels, shadows, scenery, detached effects, or chroma-colored details.
