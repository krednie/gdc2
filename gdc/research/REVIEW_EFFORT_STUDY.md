# Does the chronology actually reduce work?

Design note, 2026-11-09. No participants recruited, no outreach sent, and no study results claimed.

## Why this test is necessary

The current technical evidence separates accessible sources, faithful interpretation, useful coverage, and faithful presentation. None establishes that a professional finishes the assignment faster or produces better work.

A [randomized generative-search experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06435v1) found that references could increase trust even when invalid. The final sample comprised 4,927 US participants; this was not a professional newsroom study. Methods, results, and limitations were inspected. Clicks and time on a task page were interaction measures, not direct proof of successful verification. Therefore Alithia should not optimize perceived confidence, source clicks, or satisfaction as substitutes for correct final work.

## Product hypothesis

For a recurring professional background-research assignment, a chronology with inspectable evidence and explicit unresolved questions reduces time to an acceptable deliverable without hiding omissions or introducing unsupported certainty.

This hypothesis leaves architecture open. A well-prompted general model, an existing document tool, or a spreadsheet-assisted process is allowed to win.

## Smallest informative study

Recruit three to five consenting practitioners for formative work, not a statistically powered superiority claim. Use public or appropriately redacted materials. No confidential source communications, live allegations about private people, or sensitive personal records are needed.

Have each person reconstruct a recently completed assignment: what they started with, what they delivered, sources inspected, costly surprises, and how the work was reviewed. Record observed steps separately from retrospective estimates. Select a recurring task rather than persuading a participant to use our invented workflow.

For a subsequent task comparison, use equivalent but different event families to avoid learning the answer in the first condition. Counterbalance condition order. Keep the brief, time limit, available documents, and expected deliverable comparable. A small pilot tests feasibility and surfaces failure modes; a later powered study needs its own design.

Conditions:

- Existing workflow with access to the same sources.
- A strong AI-generated chronology with ordinary citations.
- The same candidate chronology with source passages, unresolved questions, and the proposed review controls.

The third condition should initially reuse the second condition's content. That isolates presentation/review assistance rather than confounding a better interface with a better model. A separate end-to-end study can compare generated content later.

## What to record

Time from task start to accepted deliverable; essential events retained; unsupported claims remaining; incorrect temporal or attribution relationships; legitimate claims incorrectly removed; and corrections performed by the user.

Also log source opening and navigation, but treat them as explanatory behavior. Fewer clicks might mean efficient verification or failure to check. Longer review may be productive error detection or waste. Determine which from the final artifact.

Record rejected suggestions and reasons. A system that surfaces ten speculative gaps and finds one useful record may impose more work than it saves. Count review of rejected suggestions in total effort.

Ask the participant to explain why the dossier is ready. Compare that explanation with remaining errors. Confidence should be calibrated to quality rather than merely increased.

Have a reviewer who did not know the condition assess final artifacts against a source-backed rubric. If this independence is unavailable, label the study formative and avoid a comparative performance claim. Preserve disagreements over what is essential.

## Repeat-use and purchasing evidence

After the task, observe whether the person voluntarily uses the workflow on another real assignment, retains the output, edits it, or exports it into their normal system. Ask what they would stop using, who would pay, and which approval/security steps a purchase would require.

Do not interpret an enthusiastic demo reaction, hypothetical price, or free trial signup as willingness to pay. Do not set subscription pricing until task frequency and value are observed. A low-frequency task may support a utility or integration rather than a destination platform.

## Stop or redirect

If ordinary cited output performs as well, simplify. If acquisition dominates, improve access to a narrow useful source class. If interpretation dominates, test targeted checks. If useful coverage dominates, improve task-specific selection and search. If review controls add effort without improving final work, remove or redesign them.

If professionals do not return despite technically better outputs, revisit the task and distribution rather than building a larger extraction stack.

## Founder question at this stage

Would I want my AI to generate another ambitious architecture, or show whether anyone's work gets meaningfully better? The next external evidence needed is an observed task and a repeat-use decision. Until then, keep the product hypothesis provisional and the research artifacts reusable.
